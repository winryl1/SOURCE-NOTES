**Speech styles** indicate a level of authority with which a person speaks. When a person interacts with others, he or she uses a particular style of speaking. According to Ronald Wardhaugh:
> no one speaks the same way all the time. 

You will find yourself using different speech styles in different situations. 
# Factors 
1. Audience
2. Topic: 
3. Purpose
4. Location

# Types 
## Intimate
This occurs when there is **no social inhibition** between the communicators. This speech style usually is done between people who are **very close** with each other. The communicators have a **completely personal vocabulary** that they only can understand. 
### Features 
1. Private Conversations 
2. Participants use short, incomplete utterances
3. Grammar and rules are ignored 
4. Nonverbal cues 
5. Words of endearment 
## Casual 
This is a relaxed, informal dialogue between people who are close. They use a **group language** that only members of said group can understand. You may use this to make **someone feel at ease**or to **know someone on a personal level** 
### Features 
1. Conversational tone: use of slang, colloquial words, elliptical sentences, informal contractions
2. General words
3. interruptions are common
4. Nicknames are used 
## Consultative 
This is a **semi-formal** type of speech style that is **operational**—meaning it's done when one **asks for an experts opinion/expertise/advice**, thus the name "consultative". 
### Features 
1. Used in ordinary or routing transactions 
2. Business or professional situation
3. Interruptions are allowed 
4. Titles such as "Mr.", "Ms.", "Dr."
5. Careful with words
## Formal 
What distinguishes formal from [[#Consultative]] is that **lack of listener participation**. The listener does not participate in **most cases** (see [[Models of Communication#Linear]]) and is usually done in a formal situation (graduation ceremony, meeting, television cast). 
### Features 
1. No interruptions
2. Clear pronunciation
3. Grammar and rules are followed 
4. Complex Sentences structures
5. Ideas are cohesive and organized 
## Frozen 
This is the **most formal** speech style and is also called **oratorical style**. It uses a prescribed and highly formal language, such as in official or historical documents and literary texts.
