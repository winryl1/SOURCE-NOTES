**Waste** refers to discarded materials mainly from human activities. There are two main types:
1. Solid waste: 
	- Municipal: waste from a community that are no longer useful
	- Agricultural: waste derived from poultry and farming
	- Industrial: waste comes from industrial sources that is NOT mining
	- Mining waste
2. Liquid waste

# Waste Disposal 
1. Landfill: 
	- cheapest and most convenient
	- high risk of groundwater contamination
	- emit CO<sub>2</sub> and Methane
2. Incineration
	- controlled burning of trash which turns into gas
	- heat from incineration is used for energy production to drive a turbine 
	- emits harmful pollutants into the air, water, and land 
3. Mulch: the use of organic materials on top of soil to **retain moisture** and **suppress weeds**
4. Composting
	- The use compost to make the soil more fertile 
	- This is done by allowing organic materials to decompose naturally 
5. Source Reduction: minimize waste at the very beginning of the product lifecycle—from design to production to consumer use 
6. Recycling: collecting used materials and reprocessing them into new products 

# Waste Management 
**RA 9003** is a law that aims to protect the public's health and environment through proper waste disposal and environmentally-friendly methods in making use of our resources.
