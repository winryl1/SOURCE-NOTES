# Why is Earth the only one able to sustain life? 
## Distance 
Earth is located 1AU (~150,000,000km) away from the Sun, which is known as the **habitable zone,** ensuring that our planet receives just the right amount of light and heat to maintain temperatures suitable for life. This zone is neither too close to the star, where it would be too hot (i.e. Venus, Mercury), nor too far away, where it would be too cold (i.e. Jupiter, Saturn). Its distance from the Sun also creates the perfect conditions for liquid **water** to exist on its surface — a **prerequisite for life.**
## Atmosphere 
The atmosphere of Earth is primarily composed of nitrogen (78%) and oxygen (21%), with small amounts of other gases such as argon, carbon dioxide, and trace amounts of other gases. This composition is important for several reasons.

The presence of carbon dioxide and oxygen **allows for respiration and photosynthesis**, which are fundamental to life on Earth. This composition can also help the Earth **regulate its temperature and climate** through the greenhouse effect by using greenhouse gases (carbon dioxide, water vapor, methane). Without this, the Earth’s climate would be too cold and unstable to maintain lifeforms.

Additionally, this atmosphere (along with the magnetic field) creates **a protective barrier** for the lifeforms on Earth. The ozone layer filters out most of the Sun’s harmful UV rays, preventing them from causing damage to living organisms. 

The atmosphere also **facilitates the water cycle,** which helps distribute water across the planet — a stated before, a prerequisite in life. 

On the other hand, Jupiter and Saturn lack oxygen and water, which are essential for life, and are therefore unable to develop life as we understand it.
## Magnetic Field 
This magnetic field acts as a **protective shield** against harmful solar and cosmic radiation. Without this protection, life on Earth would be severely threatened by the constant bombardment of solar wind and cosmic rays. Though this shield is present in Earth, Jupiter, and Saturn, that doesn’t mean that all of these planets can provide life (other factors, as stated before, also come into play).

# Subsystems 
- [p] Our planet has **4 subsystems** that allows for the development of life: 
1. Biosphere
	- The set of **all life forms** on Earth 
	- It covers all ecosystems 
	- For majority of life on Earth, the base of the food chain is reliant upon **photosynthesis**
2. Atmosphere:
	- The gaseous layer of Earth 
	- It is primarily composed of: Troposphere, Mesosphere, Stratosphere, Thermosphere, Exosphere
	- Functionality: See also [[#Atmosphere]] 
3. Lithosphere: Refers to the Earth's layers (curst, mantle, core)
4. Hydrosphere
	- It is the total amount of water on a planet; includes water that is one the surface, underground, air 
	- About 70% of Earth is covered with water; only 3% of it is fresh
