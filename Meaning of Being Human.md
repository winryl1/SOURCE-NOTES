# Existentialists

#### **Starting Out**  
> *What does it mean to ‘truly’ live?*

#### **Learn about It!**

**Existentialism** is a 19th–20th century philosophical movement focused on:
- Human existence
- Individual freedom
- Personal responsibility
- The search for meaning

It emerged strongly after the World Wars, as people questioned traditional systems and sought authenticity in a chaotic world.

#### **Two Main Branches of Existentialism**

| Type | Belief | Key Idea |
|------|--------|---------|
| **Theistic Existentialism** | Believes in God or a higher being who gives life purpose | **“Essence precedes existence”**<br>→ You are born with a divine purpose |
| **Atheistic Existentialism** | No belief in God; humans create their own meaning | **“Existence precedes essence”**<br>→ You exist first, then create your purpose |

#### **Jean-Paul Sartre – Father of Atheistic Existentialism**
- “Man is nothing else but what he makes of himself.”
- We are **thrown into the world** without choosing our birth, family, or circumstances.
- But we are **radically free** to choose our actions → therefore, **fully responsible** for our lives.

#### **Eastern Perspective: Zen Buddhism**
- Originated from Mahayana Buddhism (Chinese *Chán*, Japanese *Zen*)
- Emphasizes **personal insight** over doctrine
- Practice: **Zazen** (seated meditation) to clear the mind and perceive reality directly

#### **Keiji Nishitani – Emptiness (Śūnyatā)**
- Japanese Kyoto School philosopher
- Argued that modern life is filled with meaningless routines
- True meaning comes from embracing **emptiness** — letting go of preconceived ideas to see the world as it truly is

> “Strip your mind of bias → experience reality authentically.”

   > It believes a higher being (God) created humans with a pre-defined purpose. Life’s meaning lies in discovering and fulfilling God’s will. Essence comes before existence.

2. **How does atheistic existentialism answer these questions?**

### **Lesson 2: The Search for Meaning**

#### **Starting Out**  
> *What do I want in life?*

#### **Learn about It!**

Everyone searches for meaning—some briefly, others their whole lives.

Common approaches:
- **Carpe Diem** (“Seize the day”) – Live fully in the present
- **C’est la vie** (“That’s life”) – Accept life’s absurdities with grace

#### **Søren Kierkegaard – Three Stages of Life**

| Stage | Focus | Characteristics | Example |
|-------|--------|------------------|---------|
| **1. Aesthetic** | Pleasure, beauty, desire | Lives for fun, avoids boredom, lacks commitment | Binge-watching shows, impulsive decisions |
| **2. Ethical** | Duty, morality, social roles | Values honesty, loyalty, responsibility | Being a good student, keeping promises |
| **3. Religious** | Faith, obedience to God | Transcends ethics through personal relationship with God | Abraham willing to sacrifice Isaac |

> Movement between stages requires **authentic choice**, not passive drifting.

#### **Existential Nihilism**
- From Latin *nihil* = “nothing”
- Belief: Life has **no inherent meaning**
- Not necessarily depressing—many nihilists believe we can still **create subjective meaning**

> “There’s no objective purpose… but I can still make my life meaningful.”

#### **Leo Tolstoy – Meaning in Simplicity**
- Russian novelist and philosopher
- Found meaning in:
  - Quiet union with nature
  - Books and music
  - Loving relationships
  - Doing good for others

#### **Taoism – Wu Wei (Non-Doing)**
- Ancient Chinese philosophy by Lao-Tzu
- Central concept: **Tao** (The Way) – an invisible force guiding the universe
- **Wu Wei** = “non-action” or effortless action
  - Don’t force things
  - Be still, observe, flow with life
- True happiness lies within—not in external achievements

> “Stop chasing meaning. Be still. It’s already inside you.”

### **Lesson 3: Our Purpose**

#### **Starting Out**  
> *What is it all for?*

#### **Learn about It!**

To understand our purpose, we must confront **death**—the great equalizer.

All humans die. This awareness shapes how we live.

#### **Arthur Schopenhauer – Death as Return**
- German philosopher
- Viewed death as:
  - The **cessation of consciousness**
  - Like deep sleep—without waking
  - A return to the state before birth
- Saw life and death not as opposites, but as complements

> “Life is a journey toward dying.”

#### **Religious Perspectives on Death & Purpose**

| Religion | View of Death | Purpose in Life |
|--------|---------------|-----------------|
| **Christianity** | Union with God after fulfilling His will | Discover and obey God’s plan |
| **Islam** | Transition of soul to next life; test of righteousness | Prepare for afterlife through obedience |
| **Hinduism** | **Mahaprasthaana** (“Great Departure”)<br>Soul (**Atman**) reincarnates via **Samsara** | Learn lessons across lifetimes to achieve liberation |
| **Buddhism** | Also believes in **Samsara**<br>Goal: End suffering (**Dukkha**) | Follow **Eightfold Path** to reach **Nirvana** (freedom from suffering) |

##### **Buddhist Eightfold Path**
1. Right Understanding  
2. Right Thoughts  
3. Right Speech  
4. Right Action  
5. Right Livelihood  
6. Right Effort  
7. Right Mindfulness  
8. Right Meditation  

> Nirvana ≠ heaven → It’s a **state of peace and enlightenment**

#### **Secular/Naturalistic View**
- No afterlife, no reincarnation
- Death is the **final end**
- No objective purpose given by a higher power
- BUT: Humans can **create their own purpose** through relationships, goals, art, service

> “If there’s only one life, make it count.”

#### **Who’s That Philosopher?**
**Arthur Schopenhauer**  
- Pessimistic worldview  
- Influenced Nietzsche and Freud  
- Believed will drives all action

### **Lesson 4: Your Purpose**

#### **Starting Out**  
> *Why am I here?*

#### **Learn about It!**

Meaning isn’t found—it’s **created** through self-reflection and action.

#### **Martin Heidegger – Authenticity and Dasein**
- Introduced **Dasein** (German: “being-there”)
- Refers to human beings as conscious entities capable of questioning their own existence
- To live **authentically**, we must:
  - Examine our lives
  - Make deliberate choices
  - Not just follow tradition or societal expectations

> “The unexamined life is not worth living.” – Socrates

Living authentically means knowing **why** you do what you do.

#### **Albert Camus – The Myth of Sisyphus**
- French-Algerian writer and philosopher
- Used the myth of **Sisyphus** as a metaphor for human struggle
  - Punished by gods to roll a boulder uphill forever
  - Each time it reaches the top, it rolls back down
- Camus saw Sisyphus as a **hero** because:
  - He continues despite futility
  - He finds dignity in effort
  - He accepts his fate with courage

> “One must imagine Sisyphus happy.” – Camus

We are all like Sisyphus—facing repetitive struggles. Yet, we keep going. That perseverance **is** meaning.

#### **Four Paths to Purpose (Based on Virtues)**

| Virtue Sought | How Purpose Is Found | Examples |
|---------------|------------------------|----------|
| **Pleasure & Happiness** | Pursuing passions, dreams, enjoyment | Travel, hobbies, creative work |
| **Honor & Courage** | Achieving success, building legacy | Career excellence, leadership |
| **Wisdom & Knowledge** | Seeking truth, understanding the world | Studying, teaching, research |
| **Charity & Love** | Helping others, fostering relationships | Volunteering, activism, caregiving |
#### **Philosophy Applied**
> What brings **you** meaning?
> - Passion? Peace? Success? Service?
>
> Reflect:
> - How does your current lifestyle reflect your purpose?
> - Are you living intentionally?
> - What changes can you make?

Your purpose doesn’t have to be grand. It just has to be **yours**.

#### **Who’s That Philosopher?**
**Albert Camus**  
- Absurdist philosopher (not strictly existentialist)  
- Key work: *The Myth of Sisyphus*  
- Believed life is inherently absurd—but we can still rebel with joy
