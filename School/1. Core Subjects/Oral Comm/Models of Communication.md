There are different models of communication that explain different systems/processes of communication. 
# One-way
## Linear
A communication model that **does not require feedback** (passive receiver). This involves a sender giving out information that the receiver absorbs. This model was introduced by **Warren Weaver** and **Claude Shannon**.

> [!example]- Examples
> - TV Report 
> - Public Speeches
> - Radio
# Two-way
## Interactive 
A communication model where the participants go on a "**back-and-forth process**" with each other. This model involves a **feedback** but it is delayed and comes after the initial message. This model was introduced by **Wilbur Schramm**.
> [!example]- Examples
> - Email 
> - Text Messages 
> - Forums 
> - Q&A in classrooms 
## Transactional 
This model is similar to interactive but occurs **simultaneously**. This happens when communication is **ongoing and dynamic** (in other words, real-time). People can exchange messages at once, verbal or non-verbal. This also accounts for individual differences and contexts that influence the message. This model best describes how we communicate with others, especially people we are closest to. This model was introduced by **Dean Barnland**.
> [!example]- Examples
> - Video Call
> - Live and Casual conversation
