# Regulatory 

> [!info] Definition
Communication can be used to **regulate/control** the flow of conversations. It can also be used to organize a community. It prevents interruptions, awkward silences, and ensures smooth interaction.

## Verbal Cues
- “That’s it.” / “Thank you.” → speaker finished
- “Yeah.” / “Uh-huh.” → encourage continuation
- “Um...” / “Well...” → speaker not finished or uncertain
- “Do you follow?” / “Is that clear?” → check flow of conversation
## Nonverbal Cues
- **Eye Contact**
	- Speaker looks at listener → requests feedback / passes floor
	- Listener looks up at speaker → wants to take floor
	- Speaker avoids eye contact → suppresses listener
- **Head-shaking** → listener wants speaker to stop
- **Index finger raised** → speaker not done
- **Leaning back** → finished, yielding the floor
# Social Interaction 
> [!info] Definition
This is communication's **primary function**: to interact with other people and form connections. It is the dynamic exchange of messages.

> [!example]- Types
> 1. Exchange: doing something for a **reward**
> 2. Cooperation: acting together to achieve a **common goal**
> 3. Competition: **working against** another group to achieve a **common goal**
> 4. Conflict: clashing interests 
> 5. Accommodation: **middle ground** of cooperation and conflict; compromising/sacrificing to achieve cooperation and resolution
# Motivation
Communication serves to **inspire and encourage action**.  
It is a driving force for achieving goals.

### Benefits of Motivation
- **Increases productivity** → tasks are done more effectively.
- **Empowers people** → builds confidence and achievement.

### Ways to Motivate Through Communication
1. **Walk the talk** → be motivated yourself first.
2. **Be an active listener** → understand others’ goals.
3. **Remind them they make a difference** → recognize contributions.
4. **Communicate clearly and compassionately** → avoid misinterpretation.

### Example Dialogue
- Rina comforts Carla after she got a poor essay mark.  
- Uses verbal cues (“You can do it!”) and nonverbal cues (pat on shoulder, hug).

### Motivation in Different Settings
- **Classroom** → teacher motivates students.
- **Clubs/Organizations** → leader motivates members.
- **Workplace** → manager motivates employees.
- **Public Speaking** → motivational speeches inspire change.

**Example: Steve Jobs (Stanford Commencement 2005)**  
- Trust the process: “You can only connect the dots looking backward.”  
- Live authentically: “Don’t waste your life living someone else’s.”  
- Famous closing: *“Stay hungry. Stay foolish.”*
# Information
Communication is a process of **sharing and obtaining knowledge**.

### Importance
- Transmits ideas, observations, and thoughts.
- Builds relationships.
- Shapes perception.
- Influences others.

### Providing Information
Tips:
- Make eye contact, speak clearly.
- Vary intonation and pauses.
- Focus on relevant details.
- Ensure information is **reliable, accurate, clear, and complete**.

### Receiving Information
Tips:
- Take down notes (only key points).
- Ask Wh-questions (“Who, what, where, why, when, how”).
- Ask for clarification and verification information 

### Example Dialogue
- Tim asks librarian for books about airplanes.
- Librarian provides guidance to encyclopedias.
- Communication = giving + obtaining information.
# Emotional Expression
Communication allows humans to **express and manage emotions** through verbal and nonverbal means.

### Key Points
- Emotions affect communication flow.
- Scientific studies suggest 4 basic emotions:  
  **Happy, Sad, Afraid/Surprised, Angry/Disgusted**.
- Expressed via voice, facial expressions, and body movements.
- Proper expression prevents miscommunication.

### Common Emotions & Expressions

#### **Happy**
- Situations: winning, reunions, celebrations.
- Nonverbal: smiling, laughing, enthusiastic tone, “high five.”
- Expressions:  
  - “That is good news!”  
  - “Congratulations!”  
  - “I feel on top of the world!”

#### **Sad**
- Situations: failure, loss, heartbreak.
- Nonverbal: drooping eyes, low posture, crying, sighing.
- Expressions:  
  - “I feel so sad about it.”  
  - “I’m devastated.”  
  - “I’m a little down today.”

#### **Surprised**
- Situations: unexpected events.
- Nonverbal: wide eyes, raised brows, dropped jaw.
- Expressions:  
  - “Really? I can’t believe it!”  
  - “Wow! I did not see that coming.”  
  - “Oh my gosh!”

#### **Angry**
- Situations: insult, betrayal, frustration.
- Nonverbal: clenched fists, teeth grinding, loud voice.
- Expressions:  
  - “What were you thinking?!”  
  - “I’m sick and tired of this!”  
  - “I am so pissed right now!”
