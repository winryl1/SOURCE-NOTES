Energy is the capacity to do work. According to the **Law of Conservation of Energy**, energy cannot be created/destroyed, only transformed.
    
- **Electrical energy** = movement of electrons in a conductor.
    
- **Power** = rate of doing work, unit: Watt (Joule/second).
    
# Energy Sources
## Renewable 
Energy sources that can be quickly replenished quickly.
#### Geothermal Energy
- Internal heat of Earth.
- Requires: **heat source (magma), reservoir (permeable rock with water), and geothermal fluid**.
- Boreholes drilled → steam → turbines → electricity.
- Philippines = 2nd largest geothermal energy producer (after USA).
- Sites: Tiwi (Albay), Mt. Makiling-Banahaw, Leyte, Mt. Apo, Negros, Sorsogon.

#### Hydroelectric Energy
- Energy from flowing water.
- Components: reservoir, dam, penstock, turbines, powerhouse.
- Types:
    1. **Impoundment** – with dam.
    2. **Diversion/run-of-river** – no dam, uses elevation drop.
    3. **Pumped storage** – pumps water uphill for later use.
- PH Examples: Magat (Isabela), Angat (Bulacan), Agus (Mindanao).
#### Solar Energy
- Captures sunlight via:
    - **Photovoltaic cells (solar panels)** → convert sunlight directly into electricity.
    - **Concentrated Solar Power (CSP)** → mirrors focus sunlight → heats fluid → steam turbine.
- Leading global users: China, Japan, Germany, USA, Italy.
- PH Example: ISLASOL & SACASOL (Negros Occidental).
#### Wind Energy
- Uses wind turbines, often in **wind farms**.
- Onshore near coastlines, flat areas.
#### Biomass
- Oldest energy source.
- Uses biological material (plants, wood, dung, crop waste).
- Renewable but needs sustainable management.
## Nonrenewable
Energy sources that take millions of years to form.
### Fossil Fuels
This is formed from dead living matter that was decomposed, compressed, and transformed by heat & pressure. In 2013, the Philippines 61.25% of energy consumption comes from fossil fuels.
Some Coal/diesel plants in the PH are Masinloc, Cebu Energy, Cotabato Light, etc.
#### Coal
- Formed from plant material in swamps → peat → lignite → sub-bituminous → bituminous → anthracite (highest carbon).
- Burned to heat water → steam → turbines → electricity.
- Problems: mining hazards, environmental scars, air pollution (SO₂, CO₂ → acid rain).
#### Petroleum (oil)
- From microscopic aquatic organisms (algae, plankton).
- Extracted by drilling, refined into gasoline, diesel, jet fuel            
- Major global energy source.
#### Natural Gas
- Mostly methane.
- Extracted like oil, transported in pipelines.
- Cleanest fossil fuel (less emissions than coal/oil).    
#### Nuclear Energy
- Produced by **fission (splitting atoms)**, mainly uranium-235 and thorium-232.
- Fuel rods generate heat → water boils → steam turbines → electricity.
- Problems: risk of meltdown, radioactive waste disposal.


# Water Resources
Water is essential for life (drinking, hygiene, food production, industry). Water cycle connects atmosphere, lithosphere, biosphere.

## Distribution of Water on Earth
- **Total water = constant (water budget).**
- Oceans: **97.5% (saline, not directly drinkable)**.
- Freshwater: **2.5%**
    - Glaciers/ice caps: 68.7%    
    - Groundwater: 30.1%    
    - Surface water & atmosphere: small fraction
## Types of Water Reservoirs

### Oceans
- Cover 71% of Earth.
- Key role in climate regulation.
- Desalination = process of converting saltwater to freshwater.
### Surface Water
- **Streams & Rivers** → drainage basins, tributaries, watersheds.
- **Lakes & Ponds** → collect water in depressions.
- **Wetlands** → marshes (grasses), swamps (trees), estuaries (mix freshwater & seawater).
- **Floods** → natural but worsened by deforestation & poor land use.
### Groundwater
- Stored in porous, permeable of rocks.
- Zones:
    - Unsaturated zone = partly filled.
    - Saturated zone = fully filled → **aquifers**.
- Aquifers:
    - **Unconfined** → open to surface.
    - **Confined** → trapped between impermeable layers.
- Extraction: wells, pumps, artesian wells.



## Human Impacts on Water Resources
- **Population growth** → higher demand.
- **Urbanization** → more water use & pollution.
- **Food security demands** → irrigation stress.
- **Pollution** → industrial, agricultural runoff, sewage.
- **Deforestation & mining** → acid mine drainage, ecosystem loss.
## Water Resource Management
### Organizations
- **Global orgs**: UNEP, Water.org, WATERisLIFE.
- **Philippines**:
    - DPWH/MWSS → water supply in NCR.
    - DA-BSWM → land & water management.
    - DA-BFAR → aquatic resources.
    - DOTC-PCG → prevents ocean dumping.
    - Private: Maynilad (West NCR), Manila Water (East NCR).
### Laws & Policies in PH
1. **PD 424 (1974)** – created National Water Resources Council (NWRC).
2. **PD 1067 (1976)** – Water Code of the Philippines.
3. **EO 222 (1995)** – National Water Conservation Plan.
4. **RA 8041 (1995)** – National Water Crisis Act.
5. **Clean Water Act (2004)** – water quality management.
