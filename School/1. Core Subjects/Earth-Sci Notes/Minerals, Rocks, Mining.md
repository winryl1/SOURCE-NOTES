# Minerals 
**Minerals** are naturally occurring **building blocks of rocks** (mineraloids). For something to be considered a mineral, it **must meet several criteria**:
1. Inorganic: not the product of life processes.
2. Natural Occurring
3. Definite chemical composition
4. Orderly crystal structure 
5. Solid 
## Mineral Properties 
### Optical 
1. **Luster**: ability to reflect light off of the surface 
	- Metallic:
	- Submetallic:
	- Non-metallic: 
2. **Color**: color of the SOLID mineral
	- Idiochromatic: one permanent color 
	- Allochromatic: colors change due to *impurities*
	- Pseudochromatic: "false color" due to *optical effects*
3. **Streak**: color of the mineral in powdered form 
### Crystal Habit 
It is the common or characteristic shape of a mineral. It has **several** types: 

| Prismatic   | Pencil like               |
| ----------- | ------------------------- |
| Dendiritic  | Plant-like                |
| Pyroclastic | From a volcanic explosion |
| Blocky      | Rectangular shape         |
| Tabular     | Tablet-like               |
| Bladed      | knife-like                |
| Acicular    | Needle-like               |
| Fibrous     | Furry-like                |
### Specific Gravity
It is the weight of a mineral to the weight of an **equal volume of water** at a specified temperature (typically 4°C), and is expressed as a unitless ratio.
### Strength
1. **Hardness**: the ability to resist abrasion 
	- Uses Mohs scale (Talc — softest; Diamond — Hardest)
2. **Cleavage**: tendency to break along flat, smooth planes
	- Appearance: produces flat, reflective surfaces 
3. **Fracture**: tendency to break irregularly
	- Appearance: produces rough, jagged, or curved surfaces
## Mineral Groups 
1. Silicate: contains Si + O atoms
2. Sulfate: sulfide + 4 O atoms
3. Sulfide: contains sulfate ion (S<sup>-2</sup>)
4. Halides: halogen ion + one/two elements
5. Carbonates: contains C atoms 
6. Native Metals 
# Rocks 
It is any solid that is a group or mass of minerals, pre-existing rocks, or mineral-like matter. There are three main types 
## Metamorphic 
A type of rock that comes from existing rocks (igneous, sedimentary, or even other metamorphic rocks) that are altered **MORE** by heat, pressure, or chemical fluids — without melting. There are two types of metamorphism: 
1. Regional Metamorphism
	- occurs when **high pressure and temperature** affects large areas (pressure is more dominant)
	- Produces **foliated rocks** (distinct layers)
2. Contact Metamorphism
	- occurs when heat from nearby lava/magma; pressure is lower
	- Produces **non-foliated rocks** (no distinct layers)

## Igneous 
A type of rock that is a result of cooling and solidification of magma/lava. 
### Classification by Origin 
1. **Extrusive**: Rocks that are a result of *solidifying lava* on the surface (volcanic)
	- Results in smaller or no crystals (faster cooling)
2. **Intrusive**: Rocks that are a result of *solidifying magma* under the surface 
	- Results in larger crystals (slower cooling)
### Classification by Texture 
1. Aphanitic: fine grain
2. Phaneritic: coarse grain
3. Vesicular: has voids/spaces from gas bubbles
4. Amygdaloidal: vesicular rock with minerals inside voids
5. Porphyritic: a large crystal embedded in a matrix of smaller crystals 
6. Pegmatitic: rocks with minerals greater than 3cm
### Classification by Color & Content
1. Felsic-granitic: very-light colored; >65% silica
2. Intermediate-Andesitic: gray colored; 55-65% silica
3. Mafic: dark gray colored; 45-55% silica
4. Ultramafic: very dark colored; <45% silica
## Sedimentary 
A type of rock that resulted from the **diagenesis**—a change in physical (lithification), chemical, and biological changes that occur in sediments **after deposition**. It has **three** types: 
1. Clastic: from fragments of pre-existing rocks and minerals 
2. Bioclastic: from fragments of organic organisms 
3. Biochemical: **chemically precipitated** rock (with some help from organic organisms)

# Mining 
It is the act of extracting mineral resources/ores (profitable rock or mineral) from ore deposits. There are **several steps** when mining: 
1. Prospecting — target places with potentially high amounts of ores
2. Evaluation — conducting more studies to ensure prospect areas 
3. Development — once confirmed, site development begins
4. Exploitation — extracting resources for 10-15 years 
5. Reclamation — rehabilitation process after extracting resources 

## Types of Mining 
### Surface Mining 
This type of mining occurs **close to the Earth's surface**. It has several subsets: 
1. Open-Pit Mining: digging a large open pit to extract ore 
2. Strip Mining
3. Quarrying 
4. Mountain Top Removal 

### Underground Mining 
This type of mining occurs **deep in Earth's surface**. It has several subsets: 
1. Drift Mining 
2. Slope Mining 
3. Shaft Mining
