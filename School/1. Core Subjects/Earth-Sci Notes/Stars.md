**A star** is a ball of burning gas (mostly hydrogen and helium) that is held together by its gravity. **The sun** is the closest star to earth and is our main source of heat and light energy. Without it, life on earth would be impossible. 

**Characteristics**
1. *Size* - Can affect the brightness because luminosity is directly proportional to r<sup>3</sup> 
2. *Mass* - Can affect the temperature 
3. *Color* 
4. *Brightness* - Described in terms of luminosity and magnitude; depends on composition and distance 
5. *Surface Temp* - 2500 to 50,000 K; helps in determining the color of the star

# Life Cycle of Stars 
1. Stars originate from a Stellar Nebula. Gravity pulls hydrogen, and spins faster and gets hotter, becoming a protostar. 
2. When the Protostar reaches a temperature of 15m C, nuclear fusion occurs and turns hydrogen --> Helium. 
3. This fusion releases tremendous amounts of energy that pushes against gravity. This is called the **main sequence,** where stars spend the majority of their lives. 
4. When hydrogen depletes, the core compresses while the outer shell expands creating a red giant/supergiant. In this phase, helium fuses into heavier elements.
	- Low/Medium-mass stars: turn into Red Giant 
	- Large-mass stars: turn into a Super Red Giant 
5. Once helium depletes:
	- Red giants: turn into a planetary nebula --> White Dwarf --> Black Dwarf 
	- Super Red Giants: explode in a supernova --> Nebula --> and becomes either a Neutron Star or Black whole

# Constellations 
These are star patterns which form mythological characters and creatures. However, not all star patterns are not actually constellations but rather just a part of it. **Asterism** is prominent pattern of stars that is not a constellation but rather just a part of it. 
## Visible Constellations (North of Equator)
1. Ursa Major and Minor 
2. Cassiopeia 
3. Cepheus 
4. Draco
## Visible Year-round (Southern Hemisphere)
1. Carina
2. Centaurus 
3. Crux
## Circumpolar Constellations 
They can be observed year-round, but some constellations may be observed seasonally.

# Star Maps 
These are used to show the apparent positions of the stars as seen on earth. Their position in the night skies rotates from east to west which makes them appear to rise from the east.

1 Day = 1 degree rotation
30 days = 30 degree rotation
1 year = 360 degree rotation
