Modern work culture and lifestyle has caused inactive and unhealthy practices, with almost **90% of Filipino youth** lack physical activity. Experts from DOH suggest that teenagers must perform **moderate to vigorous** physical activities for at least 30 minutes to an hour for most days of the week. Even then, Filipino teenagers considered **among the most sedentary** in the world (WHO, 2007).

According to the **Philippine National Guidelines on Physical Activity (PNGPA)**, daily physical activity prescription for teenagers is as follows: 
- Actively perform daily tasks like commuting and chores 
- Engage in exercise, dance, or sports for 40mins 
- Perform aerobic activities for 20mins 
- Engage in strength training 2-3x a week.
# Physical Activities & Exercise 
**Physical fitness** is the ability of the body to work efficiently without feeling fatigue. **Physical activities** refers to the body movement that is carried out by muscles and consumes energy. **Exercise** refers to the structured body movement along with conscious breathing, performed regularly for physical fitness. (See[[#Health-Related]] ). 

But to keep exercise safe, one must know oneself. The **Physical Activity Readiness Questionnaire (PAR-Q)** is used to assess whether a person is capable of doing certain physical activities. 
## Types of Exercise (based on energy systems)
### Aerobic 
Aerobic exercises are low to moderate intensity exercises that can be sustained for long periods. This primarily uses [[Energy Transfers and Producers#🔁 Aerobic System]]. 
> [!info]- 
>  For benefits, see[[#Cardiovascular Endurance]] 
### Anaerobic 
Anaerobic exercises are high-intensity, explosive movements that can only be sustained for short periods. This primarily uses [[Energy Transfers and Producers#Anaerobic Systems]].  
## Components 
### Warm-Up
This is essential before any workload, as it **prepares the body** for more strenuous activity. 
#### Key Concepts 
- Duration 6-10min
- Intensity: Moderate 
- Activity: General multi-joint movement, [[#^dynamic-stretching]], specific movements that mimic the actual exercise 
- 5-min recovery is recommended after warm-up
#### Key Functions 
- Increases blood flow without abrupt increase in lactic acid accumulation.
- Prepares the heart, muscles and joints by increasing responsiveness and reducing stiffness
- Enhances proprioception/spatial awareness
### Exercise Load 
This is the **actual workout session** where the body is challenged to improve its [[#Health-Related]] functionality. The load is **determined by FITT**: 
- Frequency (How often?)
- Intensity (How hard?)
- Time (How long per session?)
- Type of activity
### Cool Down
This will help the body **transition to a resting state**. This is **almost as crucial** as the actual workout. 
#### Key Concepts 
- Duration: 10min
- Intensity: Low
- Activity: Multi-join exercise (gentle jogging, walking), [[#^static-stretching]] 
#### Key Functions 
- Prevents blood from pooling in the lower limbs and a sudden drop in heart rate and BP 
- Aids in removal of metabolic waste products from the muscles and sends them to organs for removal 
- Replenishes energy consumed 
- Helps release muscle tension and promote relaxation (physical and mental)
## Benefits 
Physical activities and exercises don't just provide physiological but also psychological benefits. Research has shown that regular exercise proves to be an instant mood booster as the body is designed to make movements. Not only that, but they can also help control stress, anxiety, and depression, and enhance self-esteem and confidence. 
# Fitness Types 
## Health-Related
### Body Composition 
This refers to proportion of body fat and body mass. A healthy body has **less fat** and **more body mass**.
### Cardiovascular Endurance 
This is the ability of the respiratory system of the body to **work and supply oxygen efficiently** during a sustained activity or exercise. Cardiovascular exercises include: jogging, brisk walking, cycling, swimming, running, etc. 
#### Benefits 
- Maintains BP 
- Reduces stress & risk of chronic diseases 
- Strengthens immune system 
- Improves concentration 
- Efficient at burning fat (good for weight management)
### Muscular Strength and Endurance 
Muscular strength is the ability of a particular muscle group to **exert maximal force in a single effort.** Muscular endurance is the ability of the muscle to **sustain repeated muscle activity** without fatigue. Some exercises include: weightlifting, pull/push ups, planks, etc.

This is also known as **resistance training**. 
#### Types of Strength
##### Isometric 
1. Static: involve static muscle contractions without visible movement, where muscle length and joint angle remain constant, such as in a wall sit or plank position.
##### Plyometric
1. Explosive: rapid generation of force (quick bursts)
2. Dynamic: force applied repeatedly over a long time (linked to endurance)
#### Benefits 
- Increase in bone density (increase in mineral content) and metabolism
- Improves body shape & balance 
- Reduces risk of injury
- Increase in strength/Hypertrophy (increase in contractile proteins)
### Flexibility
This is the **ability of the joints** to move through an unrestricted, pain-free range of motion. It focuses on the muscles and bones surrounding the joins. Some exercises include: yoga, dynamic/static stretching, bends, etc. 
#### Types of stretching exercises
1. Static: the muscles are stretched to a point, and the position is held  ^static-stretching
2. Ballistic: involves or pushing the range of motion of the limbs
3. Dynamic: involves slow and repetitive motion of the limbs ^dynamic-stretching
#### Benefits 
- Great range of motion, allowing for easier movement 
- Improves balance and stability
