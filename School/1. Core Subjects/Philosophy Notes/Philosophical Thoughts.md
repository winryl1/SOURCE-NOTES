# Pre-Socratic Era 
This refers to an era of philosophical thought that precedes the [[#Classical Greek Philosophy]]. 
## Thales vs Heraclitus 
According to **Thales** (**first Greek Philosopher**), proposed that everything was made of water. But according to **Heraclitus**, the world began and with **fire**. 
## Pythagoras 
He coined the term "philosophy". He is a famous mathematician that invented the **pythagorean theorem** and believes that mathematics is universal.

## Plato
Plato is a **student of Socrates**. He coined the idea that wonder is the beginning of wisdom. 
### Famous Ideas 
#### World of Matter and Forms 
In his famous "**Allegory of the Cave**", Plato presented two views of the world. The prisoners who continued to be ignorant of anything beyond their senses reflects the view of "**World of Matter**"—which is the "world" we **perceive only with our senses**. The prisoner who escaped the cave and saw the real world reflects the "**World of Forms**"—which is the world of the eternal truths and ideas. 

This story is a metaphor for how one **moves from ignorance to knowledge**, and how **true reality** is only accessible by **intellectual effort** rather than relying solely on our senses.
#### Epistemology: Knowledge vs Opinion
According to Plato, true knowledge (episteme) is distinct from opinion (doxa). This relates to the [[#World of Matter and Forms]]. And because epistemology's focus is propositional knowledge (see [[Philosophy Concept#Epistemology]]), for it to be considered a **genuine knowledge**, it must fulfill certain conditions:
1. **Belief**: the person must assert the claim to be true; you cannot know something if you doubt it
2. **Truth**: a belief must be true in reality
	- Ex: you might believe you passed an exam, but if you actually failed, your belief is mistaken, not knowledge
3. **Justification**: there must be sufficient evidence, reasons, or proof to support the belief 
## Aristotle 
Aristotle is dubbed as the "**Father of Logic**."
### Famous Ideas 
1. [[Logic]]
2. Hierarchy of beings:
	- Minerals: no life 
	- Plants: Have life but are in a vegetative state—not being able to sense, feel, and move 
	- Animals: sentient beings; able to sense, feel, and move but not reason 
	- Humans: rational beings; man has life, senses, and reasons

# Modern Philosophy 
## Rationalism
This was championed by **Rene Descartes**, "**Father of Modern Philosophy**". It is the idea that truths are discovered through [[Logic]] and reasons.
## Empiricism
This was championed by **John Locke**, who believed that the mind is a **tabula rasa**—a blank page. And in order to fill that blank page, humans must attain knowledge through experiences and observing the world through their senses. 
## Existentialism 
This was championed by **Søren Kierkegaard**, "**Father of existentialism**". Though he did not use this term, a lot of his work laid the foundation for the movement. In the 20th century, **Jean-Paul Sartre** popularized. This philosophical thought emphasizes individual freedom, choice, and responsibility in a world without inherent meaning. It suggests that individuals create their own purpose and values (essence) through their choices and actions. The idea of existentialism can be wrapped up in one quote from Jean-Paul: 

> Existence Precedes Essence
