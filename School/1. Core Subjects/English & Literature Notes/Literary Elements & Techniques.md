# Literary Elements 
## Characters
These are the **actors that play a role in a story.** There are different type of characters depending on the basis: 
### Based on Characterization 
1. **Flat**: only shows a limited range (1-2) of personalities
2. **Rounded**: Personality develops throughout the story
3. **Stock**: stereotypical characters 
4. **Dynamic**: undergoes permanent change throughout the story
5. **Static**: remains the same throughout the story
### Based on 
1. **Protagonist:** the main character;
2. **Antagonist**: Antithesis of the main character. Everything works against them, sometimes even their own traits. 

## Setting 
This refers to the **place, time, or period** that the story takes place in. 

## Conflicts 
This is the **challenges** that the main character faces throughout the story. This is also referred to as the heart of the story. Without conflict, there can be no story. There are **7 main types** of conflict: 
1. Man vs Man 
2. Man vs Self
3. Man vs Technology
4. Man vs Nature
5. Man vs Supernatural
6. Man vs Society
7. Man vs Fate

## Plot 
This refers to the sequence of events that occur in a story. There are **2 main types**: 
1. Linear
2. Non-linear 

## Point of View (Narrator)
This is the **vantage point** of the speaker or the narrator.  There are **3 primary types**: 
1. **First Person**: the narrator recounts events from their own view; uses first-person grammar
2. **Second Person:** the narrator stats you are part of the story; uses second-person grammar
3. **Third Person:** narrator is a spectator of the story; uses third-person grammar
	- **Objective**: the narrator only reveals observable behavior
	- **Omniscient**: the narrator is **all-knowing**; can access thoughts and feelings of all characters; can provide background information
	- **Limited**: the narrator's knowledge is limited to what the **main character knows**

## Theme 
It refers to the central messages that authors are trying to convey in the text. 

# Literary Techniques 
These are used by the author to add more **depth, emotion, and complexity** to the text. 
## Figures of Speech
It plays with words **deviates/differs** from its **original meaning**.
### Tropes
#### Metaphor 
It compares two unrelated things by stating one thing is another, suggesting a **resemblance**

> [!example] 
> - "Time is a thief, stealing moments from our grasp."
> - *Explanation:* Here, time is compared to a thief to convey the idea that it takes away moments without our control.
#### Simile 
It compares two unrelated things by stating one thing **has similarities with another**. It uses signal words such as "like" or "as" when comparing. 
> [!example] 
> - Her laughter was like music, filling the room with joy.
> - _Explanation:_ In this case, the laughter is compared to music to convey its pleasant and uplifting quality.
#### Hyperbole
It is the **exaggeration of the truth** to emphasize the importance of something or to create a comic effect.
- Example:
	- I've told you a million times to clean your room!
	- _Explanation:_ In this case, the speaker is exaggerating the number of times they've asked to emphasize the frustration.
#### Personification
- Giving human attributes to non-human things 
- Example:
	- The wind whispered through the trees, sharing its secrets.
	- Explanation: Here, the wind is personified as it "whispers" and "shares secrets," attributing human-like communication to it.
#### Conflicting Words
##### Oxymoron
It pairs contradictory words to express new or complex meanings (words **MUST** be next to each other).
> [!example]
> - The silence in the empty room was deafening.
> - *Explanation*: In this example, "deafening silence" combines the contradictory ideas of loudness (deafening) and quietness (silence), which creates a new meaning and emphasizes the intense quiet nature of the empty room.

### Synecdoche
- Using a part of something to refer to the whole 
- Example
	- All hands on deck.
	- Explanation: In this example, "hands" is used to represent the whole crew or people on the ship.
### Metonymy
- Substitutes the name of one thing with the name of something closely associated with it 
- Example:
	- The White House issued a statement.
	- Explanation: In this case, "The White House" is used to represent the President or the government.
## Sound Devices
### Alliteration & Consonance
Alliteration involves the repetition of **initial CONSONANT sounds** in a series of words.

> [!example]
> - _Example:_ "Peter Piper picked a peck of pickled peppers."
> - Explanation: In this *tongue*-twister, the repetition of the "p" sound creates a rhythmic and memorable effect.
### Assonance  
- involves the repetition of VOWEL sounds within nearby words.
- Example:
	- The rain in Spain stays mainly in the plain.
	- Explanation: In this example, the long "a" sound is repeated, creating a melodic and rhythmic quality.
### Onomatopoeia
- A figure speech that explicitly evokes the actual sound of the thing they are describing 
- Example
	- The bees buzzed around the flowers.
	- Explanation: In this case, "buzzed" imitates the sound of bees.
### Anaphora 
It is the repetition of the **INITIAL** word in a text. 
## Schemes 
- Involve tinkering or changing the order, pattern, arrangement of words in a sentence to achieve an effect
- Can be broken down in helpful ways that define the sort of tinkering they employ 
- **Types:**
	1. **Repetition:** repeating words, phrases, or sounds in a particular way
	2. **Omission:** leaving out certain words or phrases that would normally be expected
	3. **Change of word order:** Shiftin around words or phrases in atypical ways
	4. **Balance:** Creating sentences or phrases equally often through the use of identical grammatical structures

# Figurative Language vs Imagery
1. Figurative Language
   - [[#Definition]] 
   - [[#Types of Figurative Language]]
     - Simile: "The stars twinkled like diamonds in the sky."
     - Metaphor: "Time is a thief."
     - Personification: "The wind whispered through the trees."
     - Hyperbole: "I have a million things to do."
2. **Imagery:**
   - **Definition:** use of vivid and descriptive language that appeals to the senses to create a mental picture  
   - **Examples:**
     - Visual Imagery: "The golden sun dipped below the horizon, painting the sky in hues of orange and pink."
     - Auditory Imagery: "The waves crashed against the shore, creating a rhythmic and soothing sound."
     - Tactile Imagery: "The soft petals of the flower felt like velvet against my fingertips."
     - Olfactory Imagery: "The aroma of freshly baked cookies filled the kitchen."
     - Gustatory Imagery: "The soup was so spicy that it burned my tongue."
