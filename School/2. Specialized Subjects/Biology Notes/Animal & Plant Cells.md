# Animal Tissues 

## Types 

### Epithelial Tissue
- **Function**: Lining, covering, protection, absorption, secretion.
- **Structure**:
  - Tightly packed cells.
  - Avascular (no blood supply).
  - Basement membrane attaches to connective tissue.
  - Apical surface may have cilia or microvilli.
- **Classification**:
  - By layers:
    - Simple (one layer)
    - Stratified (multiple layers)
    - Pseudostratified (appears layered but is not)
  - By shape:
    - Squamous (flat)
    - Cuboidal (cube-shaped)
    - Columnar (elongated)
- **Examples**:
  - Simple squamous: alveoli, capillaries (diffusion).
  - Simple cuboidal: kidney tubules (secretion/absorption).
  - Simple columnar: GI tract (absorption/secretion; has goblet cells for mucus).
  - Pseudostratified columnar: respiratory tract (ciliated; traps particles).
  - Stratified squamous: skin, mouth (protection against abrasion).
### Connective Tissue
- **Function**: Support, connection, protection, storage, transport.
- **Structure**:
  - Vascularized (except tendons/ligaments).
  - Extracellular matrix (ground substance + fibers).
  - Fibers: collagen (strong), elastin (stretchy), reticular (mesh-like).
- **Types**:
  - **Bone**: Hard matrix with calcium; support/protection.
  - **Cartilage**: Flexible matrix; types:
    - Hyaline (fetal skeleton, joints)
    - Fibrocartilage (vertebral disks)
    - Elastic (outer ear)
  - **Dense Connective Tissue**: Collagen-rich; tendons (muscle-to-bone) and ligaments (bone-to-bone).
  - **Loose Connective Tissue**: Softer; types:
    - Areolar (cushions organs)
    - Adipose (fat storage, insulation)
    - Reticular (supports lymphoid organs)
  - **Blood**: Liquid matrix (plasma); transports substances, immune function.
### Muscular Tissue
- **Function**: Contraction for movement.
- **Types**:
  - **Skeletal Muscle**:
    - Voluntary, striated, multinucleated.
    - Attached to bones.
  - **Smooth Muscle**:
    - Involuntary, non-striated, uninucleated, spindle-shaped.
    - Walls of hollow organs (e.g., stomach, blood vessels).
  - **Cardiac Muscle**:
    - Involuntary, striated, uninucleated, branched with intercalated disks.
    - Found in heart.
### Nervous Tissue
- **Function**: Irritability and conductivity (response to stimuli, signal transmission).
- **Components**:
  - **Neurons**: Basic units; consist of:
    - Dendrites (receive signals)
    - Cell body (soma)
    - Axon (transmits signals; may have myelin sheath)
  - **Neuroglia (Glial Cells)**: Support neurons.
    - CNS: Astrocytes, microglia, ependymal cells, oligodendrocytes.
    - PNS: Satellite cells, Schwann cells.
- **Neuron Types**:
  - Sensory (unipolar/pseudounipolar)
  - Interneurons (bipolar/multipolar)
  - Motor neurons (multipolar)
# Plant Tissues 
## Types
### Meristematic Tissues
These are composed of actively dividing cells; responsible for growth
#### Apical Meristems
- **Location**: Tips of roots and shoots (terminal buds).
- **Function**: Primary growth (increase in height).
- **Produces**:
  - Protoderm → Epidermis
  - Procambium → Vascular tissues (xylem & phloem)
  - Ground meristem → Ground tissues

#### Lateral Meristems
- **Location**: Along the length of stems and roots.
- **Function**: Secondary growth (increase in diameter/girth).
- **Types**:
  - **Vascular Cambium**:
    - Produces secondary xylem (inward) and secondary phloem (outward).
    - Contains ray initials (radial transport) and fusiform initials (produce vascular tissues).
  - **Cork Cambium**:
    - Produces cork (replaces epidermis in mature plants).
    - Forms periderm (cork + cork cambium).
    - Secretes suberin (waxy layer to prevent water loss).

### Permanent Tissues
These are composed of mature, non-dividing cells; provide support, storage, and transport.
#### Simple Permanent Tissues
These are composed of mature, non-dividing cells; provide support, storage, and transport.
##### Epidermal Tissue
- **Origin**: Protoderm.
- **Function**: Protection, prevention of water loss.
- **Features**:
  - Covered by cuticle (waxy layer).
  - Root hairs (increase absorption).
  - Trichomes (reduce water loss, reflect light).
  - Stomata (on lower leaf epidermis; gas exchange regulated by guard cells).

##### Ground Tissues
- **Function**: Photosynthesis, storage, support.
- **Types**:
  - **Parenchyma**:
    - Thin primary walls; no secondary walls.
    - Photosynthesis (in leaves), storage (in roots/stems).
    - Can divide under certain conditions (e.g., wound repair).
  - **Collenchyma**:
    - Unevenly thick primary walls; no lignin.
    - Provides flexible support (e.g., young stems, petioles).
  - **Sclerenchyma**:
    - Thick secondary walls with lignin.
    - Provides rigid support.
    - Types:
      - Sclereids (e.g., gritty fruit flesh, seed coats).
      - Fibers (e.g., rope, flax).
#### Complex Permanent Tissues

##### Xylem
- **Function**: Conducts water and minerals upward.
- **Cell Types**:
  - **Tracheids**: Elongated cells with pits; water transport.
  - **Vessel Elements**: Perforated plates; efficient water flow.
- **Features**: Thick secondary walls; provides structural support.

##### Phloem
- **Function**: Transports sugars, nutrients, and organic compounds.
- **Cell Types**:
  - **Sieve-Tube Elements**:
    - Lack nucleus/ribosomes; form sieve tubes.
    - Sieve plates with pores regulate flow.
  - **Companion Cells**: Support sieve-tube elements.
- **Flow**: Bidirectional (source to sink).

## Vascular Bundles
- **Arrangement**:
  - Roots: Stellar arrangement.
  - Stems:
    - Monocots: Scattered.
    - Dicots: Circular.
## Key Points
- **Primary Growth**: Apical meristems → height increase.
- **Secondary Growth**: Lateral meristems → diameter increase.
- **Epidermis**: Protected by cuticle; contains stomata and guard cells.
- **Ground Tissues**: Parenchyma (storage/photosynthesis), Collenchyma (flexible support), Sclerenchyma (rigid support).
- **Vascular Tissues**: Xylem (water/minerals upward), Phloem (sugars/nutrients bidirectional).
