# Socratic Method 
This is a **form of dialectics**, which is a two-way, active process where people can exchange their views, beliefs, and arguments to arrive at the truth they would believe. Socrates also introduced the idea of **intellectual midwifery**. Socrates compared himself to a midwife, who helps others "**give birth**" to ideas that they already have within. This technique of philosophizing involves 
1. Asking a series of questions about a general belief
2. Pressing for definitions for clarity
3. **Elenchus/Cross-examination**: Testing beliefs and show their contradictions, which encourages others to reevaluate their views (intellectual midwifery)
4. **Aporia**: a state of puzzlement when the person realizes that their initial understanding of a concept is flawed. 
# Hegelian Dialectics 
Hegelian dialectics was introduced by George Wilhelm Friedrich Hegel and is similar to the [[#Socratic Method]]. It involves **contradictory aspects** within a concept, which leads to **sublation**— cancelling out the errors and limitations. It has 3 levels: 
1. **Thesis**: The current prevailing idea 
2. **Antithesis**: something that opposes the thesis 
3. **Synthesis**: a resolution of the conflict between thesis and antithesis; it takes the good parts of both and overcomes their limitations, which leads to a newer, more "concrete" concept
