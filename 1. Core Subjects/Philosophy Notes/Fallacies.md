**Fallacies** (Latin: *Faller*—Deception) are errors in logical reasoning which makes an argument invalid. Though the line/progression of reasoning may be valid, it may turn out to be invalid. 

# Types 
## Irrelevance 
A type of fallacy that does not **address the issue in question**. In this type, an argument, though potentially valid, fails to prove the conclusion that was originally at issue, and instead address a different point. 
### Ad Hominem 
A type of argument that **attacks the person** making the argument instead of addressing the argument itself. 
> [!example] 
> Don't listen to her about climate change, she failed chemistry in high school 

In this example, the person is attacking the woman instead of her argument, claiming she failed in chemistry. The person then concludes that her argument is invalid because she failed in her class. 

#### Tu Quoque
A **subtype of ad hominem** that dismisses someone's argument and **accusing** them of being **inconsistent** or **hypocritical**. 

> [!example]
> Person A: You should stop smoking cigarettes!
> Person B: You are one to talk. Why should I believe you? You used to smoke too!

In this example, person B attacks person A instead of A's argument. B frames A as a hypocrite, therefore making A's argument as invalid.
### Ad Baculum
A type of argument that **uses threats/force** to make someone accept a conclusion. This could either be social, physical, or even emotional. This fallacy rests on **fear of consequences**. 

> [!example]
> If you don't do what I say, you will regret it! 

In this example, the speaker is threatening the respondent that if he/she does not follow him, he/she will regret it. Instead of providing reasons or evidences, the speaker is using a threat to influence the responder. 

### Ad Ignorantiam
A type of argument that something must be true because it has not been proven false (and vice versa). The absence of evidence is an evidence itself.. The burden of proof lies on the making the claim, not on others to disprove it. This fallacy appeals to **ignorance**. 

> [!example] 
> Zeus exists because he hasn't been proven to NOT exist.

In this example, instead of providing evidences of Zeus' existence, the speaker claims that Zeus exists because there is no evidence suggesting that Zeus doesn't exist. This is false because we can also say that Zeus doesn't exist because there is not evidence of his existence. 
### Ad Misericordiam
A type of argument that **uses pity/sympathy** to support itself.

### Red Herring 
Introducing an irrelevant topic to distract from the real issue. 
### Strawman 
A type of argument that **misrepresents/exaggerates someone's argument** to make it easier to dismiss. 


## Presumption
A type of fallacy that occurs when an argument relies on **unjustified/unsupported** assumptions. 
### Complex/Loaded Question
A question that unfairly assumes a controversy is true, trapping the respondent. 
### False Cause 
Assumes that one thing happened before another caused the other.
### Slippery Slope 
Assumes that one small step will inevitably lead to a chain of extreme consequences. 
### Composition & Division 
These two types are polar opposites. **Composition** assumes that what is true of the parts must also be true of the whole. **Division** assumes that what is true of the whole must also be true of the parts.

## Ambiguity
A type of fallacy that occurs when an argument's wording or meaning is **unclear**, **shifting**, or **misleading**. The conclusion **rests on language confusion** instead of sound reasoning.
