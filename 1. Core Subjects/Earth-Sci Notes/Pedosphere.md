**Soil** is a layer **unconsolidated regolith** (loose rock and dust) that shits atop of bedrock. **Soil science** is concerned with:
- soil formation, classification and mapping
- physical, chemical, biological, and fertility properties

It has **two divisions**: 
1. **Edaphology**: soil as habitat for organisms
2. **Pedology**: soil as a geologic entity (formation, classification, chemistry, morphology)

# Soil Formation 
1. Soil begins as solid rock that is disintegrated by erosion and weathering 
2. As rock continues to be disintegrated, organic matter is introduced and mix with minerals
3. Organic matter helps break down parent material and add nutrients to the rock 
4. As time passes, more layers of soil form and become more complex and mature 
## Factors 
1. Parent Material: source of regolith; undergoes physical and chemical changes 
	- Soil same location as parent material: **Residual soil**
	- Material is from transported, unconsolidated deposits: **Transported soil**
2. Climate: how temp and moisture vary and determine weathering 
3. Biological Influences: how organisms (humans included) affect soil formation
4. Topography: slope and aspect affect the moisture and temp of soil
5. Time

# Soil Texture 
It is the relative proportion of particle sizes of sand, silt, and clay. Texture can affect soil porosity and water retention. 
> [!tip]
> Bigger Particles = Low Water Retention
> Smaller Particles = Higher Water Retention

| Properties              | Sandy    | Silt                               | Clay                            | Loam                  |
| ----------------------- | -------- | ---------------------------------- | ------------------------------- | --------------------- |
| Particle Size           | 0.05-2mm | 0.002-0.05mm                       | <0.002mm                        | Mixed                 |
| Nutrient Level          | Low      | High                               | High                            | High                  |
| Water Retention Ability | No       | Yes (+ air circulation)            | Yes (very difficult to drain)   | Yes                   |
| Other Properties        | Acidic   | More fertile than other soil types | Sticky when moistened; moldable | Ideal for plan growth |
# Kind of Soil
1. Clay soil: mostly clay; can become waterlogged and hard when dry
2. Silty soil: mostly silt; rich in nutrients and high moist retention
3. Sandy soil: mostly sand
4. Loam: ideal for agriculture (sand + silt + clay)
5. Peaty soil: mostly peat (dead organic matter) 
6. Chalky soil: high in calcium carbonate; derived from chalk/limestone 
7. Alkaline soil: pH above 7; caused by calcium carbonate & sodium
# Soil Profile 
The sequence of soil horizons from surface to bedrock. Here are the layers (top to bottom)
1. O (humus/organic): loose and partly decayed organic matter; habitat of microorganisms
2. A (topsoil): mineral + dark organic humus
3. E (eluviated): area of eluviation (downward movement of dissolved/suspended soil materials)
4. B (subsoil): accumulation of clay from above 
5. C (substratum): partially altered parent material 
6. R (bedrock): unweathered parent material
# Soil Types (Major Biomes)
1. Forest
	- Characterized by distinct layers and moderate organic matter
	- Soil is well developed (thick humus as evidence)
	- Dominant Soil Orders: Alfisols, Spodosols
2. Tropical
	- Exposed to extremes (high temp, high rainfall); faster weathering
	- Dominant Soil Order: Oxisols
3. Grassland
	- Agriculturally productive soils
	- Dominant Soil Order: Mollisols
