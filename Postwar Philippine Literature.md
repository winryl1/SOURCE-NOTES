# Post-War 
After WW2 and the Japanese occupation ended, the Philippine's literary scene was once again dominated by writers in English. Among these were novelists Edilberto and Edith Tiempo, short-ficitonist Francisco Arcellana, poet-critic Ricaredo Demetillo, poet-ficitonist Amador Daguio, poet Carlos Angeles, fictionists NVM Gonzalez and Bienvenido N. Santos.

## Characteristics
1. **Romanticism**: a response to **neoclassicism** which emphasized imagination and emotions
2. **Nationalism**: to exalt and give more importance to one's nation and culture above the others
3. **Independence**: The quality of having freedom after being controlled
4. **Nature**: praising the natural life 
5. **Expression of feelings**

## Author and Works
1. Macario Pineda: "Ang Ginto sa Makiling" (1946)— the first novel after WW2; a quest of the discovery of the mystery of *Makiling*
2. Stevan Javellana: "Without Seeing the Dawn" (1947) — experiences of the Filipinos after WW2
3. Nestor Vicente Madali Gonzales: "A Season of Grace" (1956) — portrayed the lives of the working class and the masses in rural areas

# Martial Law 
In Sept 21, 1972, late president Ferdinand Marcos Sr. declared Martial Law under Proclamation No. 1081. Prominent figures were arrested for criticizing the government. And as a result, many different literary works emerged, aiming to expose and narrate military abuses against the people, economic plunder by bureaucrats and cronies, and impoverishment of the masses. 

## Protest (Revolutionary)
It refers to works that express distaste, disagreement, or transgression to the present government. An example of this is the *Dekada '70* by **Lualhati Bautista**. One aspect under this type of literature was the **social realism**—which aimed to effect social change by portraying the truths of society and disparity of social classes 

Amado Hernandez, a union leader and social activitist, wrote novels like *Luha ng Buwaya* (1963) deals with the struggle between the oppressed peasantry and the class of politcally power landlords. Another works is *Mga ibong mandarangit* (1969) deals with the domination of Filipinos by American industry.

Another work by Jose Lacaba entitled *Prometheus Unbound* was published where a hidden message was shown: "MARCOS HITLER DIKTADOR TUTA". 

## Proletarian 
This refers to the literary works written by working-class authors about the struggles of the working class. 

**Arturo B. Rotor** said that while the country had a beautiful side, he also said that the reality of social injustice should be known through literary texts.

**Hernando Ocampo** wrote stories like *We or They*, depicting hungry peasants looting a rice warehouse and getting killed. 

## Prison Literature 
This refers to the works produced by authors who are incarcerated or confined in prison. During Martial Law, a lot of writers and scholars who spoke out against the regime such as Bienvenido Lumbera, Ninotchka Rosca, Ricardo Lee, and Jose Ma. Sison were arrested. 

Example of works during this period is *Pintig sa Malamig na Bakal: Peoms and Letters from Philippine Prisons* (1979) and **Mila Agular's** *Why cage pigeons* (1984).
## Circumvention
This refers to works that express social and political transgression through literary devices. Example of works are **Jose Lacaba's** *Sister Stella L.* (1984) and *Bayan Ko: Kapit sa Patalim* (1985). 

**Nicanor Tiongson's** “Hindi Ka Nag-iisa: Voices of Protest in Literature, Theater and Film” (1983-1986) borrowed a theater term from Bienvenido Lumbera. He said the “literature of circumvention” prevalent from 1972 to 1983 was the artists’ way of “talking about and speaking the truth.”
# Post-EDSA (1986-1995) 
This refers to the period when Marcos' regime ended and a new government was established. And because people no longer were suppressed, they started a new age of contemporary Philippine literature.
## Features 
1. Post-structuralist & postcolonial: 
	- Post-structuralist: argues that there is no fixed meaning and the idea of Authoritative author, proposing that the reader plays a central role in interpreting the text
	- Postcolonial: shows the situation of a country after being colonized
	- Critics such as Virgilio Almario, Isagani Cruz, and Soledad Reyes embarked on these approaches in reading Philippine Literature
2. Nontraditional projects: Anthologies, novels, poems, etc.
3. Rise of LGBT & Feminism
4. Vernacular Literature: literature that uses daily speech of common people
## Creative Writing Centers 
- Academic Institutions: included creative writing as part of their curricular offerings, which allowed students to hone their writing craft.
- Writer's organizations: periodically sponsored symposia on writing and/or set up workshops for their members and other interested parties
