**Speech acts** are things that we do with our words—not just the literal meaning, but **what we are trying to do**. 
> [!example]
> "Can you open the door?"
> You are not really asking if they *can* open the door, you asking them *to open* the door. 

**The words** = what you say out loud 
**Speech acts** = what you actually mean to do with those words 

# Speech Theory 
This regards language as an action instead of as a medium for conveying and expressing ideas. It says that words, symbols, and sentences are the building blocks of **mutual understanding**. There are two types of utterances: 
1. Constative: done to **describe a situation**. 
2. Performative: words that **incite action**.

# New Types 
## Locution
The act of **producing meaningful utterances**. It is saying something in the normal sense
### Category 
1. Utterance act: words that come out of your mouth that may not have meaning 
2. Propositional act: express a specific definable point, as opposed to mere utterances that may be unintelligible; basically, you are pointing out something or making a reference
## Illocution
This is the **meaning** one wishes **to communicate or convey**.  
### Categories
1. Expressives: expresses the emotion of the speaker 
2. Declarations: bring about change in a situation
3. Assertives: stating something you believe to true (making a claim, or giving information)
4. Directives: cause the one being addressed to do something in particular (command, request, suggestion)
5. Commissives: commit the speaker to doing something in the future 
## Perlocution 
This is the effect, intentional or not, the utterance has on the receiver.
