# D&C 82:1-7
Joseph Smith received a revelation describing the Lord's expectations of His people. In D&C 82:3, it says: 
> [!quote] D&C 82:3
> For of him unto whom much is given much is *required*; and he who sins against the greater light shall receive the greater condemnation.

The Lord talks about our discipleship. Being members of the Church, we have been blessed — or in other words "given much" — by the Lord for knowing and having the fullness of the gospel. But it doesn't stop there. The Lord requires us to share that light with others and to help "bring pass the immortality and eternal life of man" (Moses 1:39). We are to put first in our lives the things of his kingdom. We are commanded to "keep [his] commandments". We have made covenants with Him. As the Lord has said: "If you will that I give unto you a place in the celestial world, you must prepare yourselves by doing the the things which I have commanded you and required of you" (D&C 78:7).

> [!quote] Quote by: Sheri L. Dew 
> At times the demands of discipleship are heavy. But shouldn't we expect the journey towards eternal glory to stretch us? We sometimes rationalize our preoccupation with this world and our casual attempts to grow spiritually by trying to console each other with the notion that living the gospel really shouldn't require all that much of us. **The Lord's standard of behavior** will always be **more demanding than the world's**, but then the Lord's rewards **are infinitely more glorious-including true joy, peace, and salvation**. 

# D&C 82:8-24
In verses 8-10, we read that when we obey the Lord's commandments, He promises to bless us. God will not and cannot abandon his relationship with those he made covenants with. He does not pull away. God never tires to help us fulfill our end of the covenant and to be patient with us.
