# Part 1: "Building of Mine House"
In 1832, the Lord commanded the Church to build a temple. But the Church leaders did not take this seriously and focused on building other houses. And while the Lord was pleased of their enthusiasm and efforts, He pointed out that they committed a "grievous sin" for not following his commandment to build the "more important" structure—the temple.

The Lord commanded the Church to build the temple so that he could "pour out my Spirit upon all flesh... to endow those whom I have chosen with power". The Lord then promised them that if they keep His commandments, He will give them the power to build it. 

At the time, building a temple would be very difficult with very little man-power, experience, and finance. As the saints diligently kept the commandments, the Lord started to provide solutions to their problems. This points back to 1 Nephi 3:7: 

> [!quote]
> I will go and do the things which the Lord hath commanded... save, He shall prepare a way for them that they may accomplish the thing which he commandeth them.

---
The temples are the only places where heaven and earth meet. It is where we feel God's presence the most because this is God's house. In it, we can make covenants with God and perform ordinances that are essential for our salvation and exaltation. This points back to God's ultimate purpose in Moses 1:39

> [!quote]
> For behold, this is my work and my glory; to bring pass the immortality and eternal life of man. 

# Part 2: Pure in heart 
In D&C 97:21, the Lord described Zion as "pure in heart". Zion is not a place, it's a community and an environment where no unclean thing can enter. Zion is the saints of God.

To be pure in heart to love and serve the Lord will your heart, mind, and strength. It is to emulate Christ. But this does not mean that we have to be perfect. Perfect does not come in this world, but in the world to come. The Lord expects us to become increasingly pure through repentance. 

It is also through the temple that we can become more pure.
