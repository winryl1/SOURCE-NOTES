# Part 1: Forgive one another 
In D&C 64:9-11, God teaches us about forgiveness. He requires us to forgive one another. He also says to leave judgement to Him. Refusing to forgive another person is to deny that person the blessings of the Atonement. It is important to remember that we are all sinners. None of us are truly worthy to walk with God without the Atonement of Jesus Christ, and the Atonement is for everyone.

> [!quote] Quote by: Confucius
> To be wronged or robbed is nothing unless you continue to remember it.

However, it is important to note that God did not say "You are not allowed to feel pain or sorrow from heartbreaking experiences." God also did not say that we should reenter a toxic relationship to forgive one another. 

Forgiveness is possible even without reconciliation. You may choose to forgive yet still avoid placing yourself in harm's way. Forgiveness is to give peace to your heart and soul, and lead you to Christ's "path of true healing". 

# Part 2: A heart and willing mind
When we serve the Lord, He requires us to have a "heart and a willing mind". And if we are truly willing to serve the Lord, he will direct us in our paths. One of the greatest ways to serve/show our love for God is to emulate Christ's willingness to follow the will of the Father, even if it means we have to sacrifice the things that we love.

> [!quote] Quote by: Donald L. Hallstrom
> If we love the Lord with all our heart, we are willing to give Him everything we possess. …
> Having “a willing mind” connotes giving our best effort and finest thinking and seeking God’s wisdom. It suggests that our most devoted lifetime study should be of things that are eternal in nature. It means that there must be an inextricable relationship between hearing the word of God and obeying it.

# Part 3: Prepare the Way 
As members of the Church, we are called by the Lord to prepare the way for the second coming. And we do this using the keys of the priesthood (see [[D&C 84]]). 

# Part 4: A testimony of Truth 
The elders expressed concerns about Joseph's imperfect grammar and lack of formal education. They wondered if such revelations could truly be divine. The Lord reassures the elders and tells them that He hears our prayers, knows our hearts, and can give us a testimony of the truth we seek (D&C 67:1-5). 

The Lord also emphasized that Joseph Smith can be imperfect and at the same time deliver divine messages. The Lord does not call the qualified, but qualifies the called. The Lord also tells them that to receive the testimony of truth they must humble themselves and submit to Him.
