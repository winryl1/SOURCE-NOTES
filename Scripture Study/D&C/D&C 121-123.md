# Part 1: Peace Be unto Thy Soul 
In 1838, Joseph Smith and his associates were forcefully arrested as prisoners of war, and was mistreated. They were held inside a small, cold cell where there was no heat and the food was occasionally poisoned. Joseph pleaded with the Lord to deliver them and the Lord responded (D&C 121:1-10). The Lord promises to Joseph that these will be "but a small moment" and that if they "endure it well", He will exalt them on high. 

Similarly, we can also find "peace" in our lives during times of afflictions. We may not understand the reason for our suffering, but if we put our trust in the Lord, He will bring comfort to us. We have to understand that hard times happen to all people, including the righteous, and that these things "work together for your good" (see [[D&C 98-101]]). If we remain bonded to our Father in Heaven, these things can become an opportunity to grow and to surrender to God's wisdom. Like what Joseph Fielding Smith has said: 

> [!quote] Joseph Fielding Smith
> Perhaps such things have to happen—not only **can** they happen, but **have to**. As I have read the history of those days, ... I have reached to the conclusion that the hardships .... the almost universal opposition [*towards the Church*] were necessary. At any rate, they became **school teachers for our people**. They helped to **make them strong**.
