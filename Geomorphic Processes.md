This is the physical and chemical interactions between Earth's surface and the natural forces acting upon it to produce landforms. There are two main types: 
# Exogenic 
These are processes that affect the atmosphere, hydrosphere, and biosphere through **weathering**, **erosion**, **transportation**, and **deposition**. 
## Weathering 
The breakdown of rock, soil, and minerals at or near the Earth's surface
### Mechanical
Weathering that causes Physical change without chemical change 
- Frost wedging: **freezing** causes the joints to **expand and grow, causing rocks to detach** 
- Root wedging: **roots grow underground and through the cracks,** forcing the cracks to open as they grow
- Salt wedging: force exerted from **salt crystals** after **water dries** in between cracks
- Abrasion: wearing away of rock due to **friction and constant collision**
- Exfoliation: reduction of pressure on igneous rocks; causes **peeling** 
### Chemical 
Weathering that changes the chemical composition
- Dissolution: minerals dissolve in water
- Hydrolysis: water causes the minerals to break apart (see [[Condensation & Hydrolysis]]) 
- Oxidation: reaction of oxygen with minerals, causing rusts

### Factors that affect weathering 
- Climate 
- Rock type
- Rock structure
- Topography
- Time
## Erosion & Transportation
**Erosion** is the separation and removal of weathered and unweathered rocks and soil from its substrate. 

**Transport** is the process where sediments are moved along from the surface to where they are deposited. 

---
There are a few agents of erosion: 
1. Wind Erosion: occurs in flat, bare areas. Sandstorms are a common phenomenon in deserts. Results in soil loss, dryness, deterioration of soil structure, nutrients, and productivity loss
2. Glacier
3. Water: erosion agent
## Deposition 
These is what happens when the agents of erosion loses energy and sets down the sediments (see [[Minerals, Rocks, Mining#Sedimentary]]) 
# Endogenic Processes 
These are internal geological activities driven by Earth's heat and gravity, causing the creation of landforms and events. The heat comes from: 
1. Residual Heat: accretion heat from the formation of the Earth (see [[Solar System]])
2. Radioactive Decay: comes from radioactive minerals (uranium, potassium, thorium).

---
**Magmatism** is the formation, movement, and crystallization of magma within Earth. **Magma** is a molten rock composed of abundant elements.

## Formation of Magma 
1. Decompression Melting: happens when hot rock rises and loses pressure; less pressure allows the magma to melt into liquid 
2. Flux Melting: volatiles (water and CO<sub>2</sub>) causes the surrounding rock to ahve a lower melting point
3. Heat Transfer Melting: magma is generated after a hot magma rising from the mantle conducts its heat to a cooler, solid magma and causes that to melt
