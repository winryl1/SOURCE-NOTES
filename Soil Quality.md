**Soil quality** refers to soil's ability to sustain life and animal life. We need soil for:
1. For Agriculture: soil is used to produce crops and food for everyone
2. Regulating water and filtering pollutants 
	- Absorbs rainfall, reducing floods and runoff
	- It filters contaminants as water percolates underground
	- Recharges groundwater—source of drinking water for billions
3. Mineral Deposit: soils are mined for their minerals 
4. Foundation and Support 
5. Nutrient Cycling: healthy soil is more efficient in nutrient cycling (C, N, P, K, Ca, Mg)

| Soil Conditions           | Effect                                                                     |
| ------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Rich in organic matter    | High microbial activity = rapid recycling                                  |
| Good structure & aeration | Oxygen allows microbes to be efficient decomposers                         |
| Balanced pH               | Keeps all nutrients available, while avoiding toxicity or deficency        |
| Diverse biology           | If one microbe is missing, others compensate and can still cycle nutrients |
Healthy soils are important because even if they face challenges, they can still be productive. 

---
**Soil degradation** is what happens when soil quality drops that can affect all of us.  Low quality soil cannot provide goods and services required by the ecosystem. 
1. Soil Erosion (see [[Geomorphic Processes]]) 
2. Soil Compaction
	- When soil is pressed together and becomes more dense and reduces porosity (where water and air are located)
	- Reduced porosity = less air and water for microbes (slower nutrient cycling)
	- Water absorption is harder (causing it to build up in the surface and carry away topsoil) and roots cannot easily grow
3. Desertification
4. Intensive Agriculture
5. Urbanization

> [!warning] 
> **40%** of **agricultural soils** are degraded
> **33%** of **global soil** is moderately to highly degraded
