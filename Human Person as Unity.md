# I think. Therefore, I am

- **René Descartes** (1596–1650), “Father of Modern Western Philosophy,” sought indubitable truth amid widespread deception.
- He employed **Cartesian Doubt** (methodic skepticism): systematically doubting *everything* that could possibly be false.
- **Why doubt?** Our senses deceive us:
  - Juice tastes bad after brushing teeth.
  - Seashell sounds like the ocean.
  - Optical illusions trick our eyes.
- **Causal Theory**: If senses mislead us, how can we trust them to reveal truth?
- **Evil Genius Hypothesis**: What if an all-powerful deceiver (like in *The Matrix*) is fabricating your entire reality?
  - You could be dreaming, hallucinating, or living in a simulation.
- **The Indubitable Truth**:  
  > Even if deceived, the *act of doubting* proves existence.  
  > **“Cogito, ergo sum”** — *I think, therefore I am.*  
  - The self is not defined by body, senses, or memories—but by **thinking**.

#### **Philosophy Applied**  
> Truth-seeking requires honesty. Don’t base your identity on illusions or societal labels. Like Descartes, question everything—until you find what you *cannot* doubt.

#### **Who’s That Philosopher?**  
**René Descartes**  
- French philosopher, mathematician, scientist.  
- Founder of rationalism and modern philosophy.  
- Key work: *Meditations on First Philosophy*.

# Ship of Theseus
A ship is repaired over time—every plank, sail, and crew member replaced.  
  **Question**: Is it still the same ship?  
  → Applied to the self: If we change physically, mentally, emotionally—do we remain the same person?

- **Essentialism (Aristotle)**  
  - **Essential qualities**: Necessary for identity. Change them → thing ceases to be itself.  
    *Example: A knife’s blade is essential; handle material is not.*  
  - **Accidental qualities**: Changeable without altering essence.  
    *Example: A house’s color, size, material — but its essence is “a place to live.”*  
  → You remain *you* despite haircuts, weight changes, or new hobbies.

- **Bundle Theory (David Hume)**  
  - **No permanent self** exists.  
  - The “self” is just a **bundle of perceptions**: memories, emotions, thoughts, body, DNA, habits.  
  - Like a river: constantly flowing, never the same water.  
  - No “secret ingredient” holds you together.

#### **Philosophy Applied**  
> Knowing yourself is not about finding a final answer—it’s about asking better questions.  
> *“It is not really about the answers. It is more about the questions and the journey to get there.”*

#### **Who’s That Philosopher?**  
**David Hume**  
- Scottish empiricist.  
- Argued the self is a fiction—a collection of fleeting impressions.  
- Key work: *An Enquiry Concerning Human Understanding*.

# Being the Subject
- **Subject**: The active perceiver, thinker, agent who gives meaning to the world.
- **Object**: That which is perceived, acted upon, or studied.
- **Essentialism (Aristotle)**: Things have a core essence (e.g., knife = blade).
- **Existentialism (Sartre, Camus)**:  
  > **“Existence precedes essence.”**  
  - You are not born with a fixed identity.  
  - You *exist first*, then *create your essence* through choices and actions.  
  - You are **free and responsible** for defining your meaning.

#### **Being the Subject Means:**
- You perceive the world.
- You interpret experiences.
- You make decisions.
- You are an **actor**, not just a spectator.

#### **The Paradox of Subjectivity**
- You give meaning—but you can never fully define yourself.
- You live in **absurdity**: creating meaning in a world that may have none.
- You are limited: a paralyzed person cannot be an athlete—but still must find meaning *within* their limits.

#### **Philosophy Applied**  
> If you believe existence comes before essence, how do you create meaning?  
> → Through choices: relationships, work, art, service, courage.

#### **Who’s That Philosopher?**  
**Jean-Paul Sartre**  
- French existentialist.  
- “Man is condemned to be free.”  
- Key work: *Being and Nothingness*.


# Human Subjectivity
- **Søren Kierkegaard** (1813–1855), Danish Christian existentialist.
- **Fear of freedom**: Too much freedom leads to dread and “the crowd” (untruth).
- **Three Stages of Life** (Path to authentic self):

| Stage | Meaning | Characteristics | Example |
|-------|---------|------------------|---------|
| **1. Aesthetic** | Driven by pleasure, emotion, immediacy | Avoids boredom, seeks thrills, no moral compass | Binge-watching shows, impulsive relationships |
| **2. Ethical** | Chooses moral order and responsibility | Lives by principles, commitments, duty | Getting married, paying taxes, keeping promises |
| **3. Faith** | Transcends ethics through personal relationship with God | Involves paradox, risk, “leap of faith” | Abraham willing to sacrifice Isaac (Genesis) |

> **True freedom**: Not “freedom from” constraints—but **freedom to** live for something greater.

#### **Philosophy Applied**  
> Are you living for pleasure, principle, or purpose?  
> Authenticity means choosing *yourself* over the crowd.

#### **Who’s That Philosopher?**  
**Søren Kierkegaard**  
- “Father of Existentialism.”  
- Emphasized individuality, subjectivity, and faith.  
- Key work: *Fear and Trembling*.

# Being the Object
- You are not the only subject. Others perceive you as an **object**.
- **John Locke** (1632–1704): Distinguished **Primary** and **Secondary Qualities**.

| Type | Definition | Examples | Applied to Humans |
|------|------------|----------|-------------------|
| **Primary Qualities** | Inherent in the object; objective, measurable | Mass, shape, size, motion | Height, weight, blood type, DNA |
| **Secondary Qualities** | Depend on the perceiver; subjective | Color, taste, smell, sound | “Beautiful,” “kind,” “intimidating,” “charming” |

> You have no control over how others assign secondary qualities to you.

- **The Body as Medium**:  
  - Without a body, you cannot:  
    - See a sunset  
    - Taste coffee  
    - Hold a hand  
  - Your body determines your possibilities (e.g., height for flight attendant).

#### **Philosophy Applied**  
> You are both the creator of meaning (Subject) and the one being judged (Object).  
> Don’t let others’ opinions define you—but acknowledge their perspective.

#### **Who’s That Philosopher?**  
**John Locke**  
- English empiricist.  
- “Tabula rasa” — mind is a blank slate shaped by experience.  
- Key work: *An Essay Concerning Human Understanding*.


# Human Objectivity
- **Maurice Merleau-Ponty** (1908–1961): French phenomenologist.
- Studied soldier **Schneider** with brain damage:
  - Could not touch his forehead when asked → **Abstract Knowledge** failed.
  - But instinctively swatted a fly off his forehead → **Embodied Knowledge** worked.

| Type                   | Definition                                       | Example                                                                  |
| ---------------------- | ------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Abstract Knowledge** | Conscious response to commands                   | “Touch your nose.”                                                       |
| **Embodied Knowledge** | Automatic, intuitive, learned through experience | Riding a bike, typing without looking, knowing where the light switch is |

- **The World is Meaningful**:  
  - Objects aren’t just physical—they carry meaning through use and familiarity.  
  - Light switch = “turns on light.”  
  - Doorbell = “someone’s at the door.”  
- **Lived Experience** shapes how we interact with the world.

#### **Philosophy Applied**  
> Where do you feel most at home? Why?  
> Your environment holds meaning because of your embodied history with it.

#### **Who’s That Philosopher?**  
**Maurice Merleau-Ponty**  
- Emphasized the **lived body** (*corps propre*) as the center of perception.  
- Key work: *Phenomenology of Perception*.


# Being the Subject and Object

#### **Learn about It!**
- **Gabriel Marcel** (1889–1973): French existentialist and playwright.
- Criticized Descartes’ detached rationalism.  
- Introduced **Primary** and **Secondary Reflection**:

| Mode | Description | Focus |
|------|-------------|-------|
| **Primary Reflection** | Detached, scientific analysis | “My body” as an object (anatomy, biology) |
| **Secondary Reflection** | Personal, participatory awareness | “My body” as uniquely mine — the source of experience |

## The Paradox: “I have my body” vs. “I am my body”

| Statement | Meaning |
|----------|---------|
| **“I have my body”** | Body as possession: I own it, care for it, use it, abuse it. Like a car or book. |
| **“I am my body”** | Body is inseparable from self: Identity is embodied. Cannot exist apart from it. |

- **Body as Intermediary**:  
  - Through body: I experience the world (touch, taste, sight).  
  - Through body: I express myself (gestures, speech, tears).  
  - Through body: I relate to others and to God.

#### **Intersubjectivity**  
- I am not the only subject.  
- Others see me as object.  
- Yet, through bodily interaction (eye contact, touch, voice), we share meaning.  
- **Authentic relationship = Presence** — being fully embodied with another.

#### **Philosophy Applied**  
> Being a good friend, child, or citizen is not just behavior—it’s an embodied expression of your subjectivity.

#### **Who’s That Philosopher?**  
**Gabriel Marcel**  
- Christian existentialist.  
- Emphasized “being” over “having.”  
- Key work: *The Mystery of Being*.
