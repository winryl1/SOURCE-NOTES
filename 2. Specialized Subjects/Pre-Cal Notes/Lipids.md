
> [!success] Lipids Overview
> Biological molecules that contain **Carbon**, **Hydrogen**, **Oxygen**; similar to [[Carbohydrates]] but in **lower proportions**. It is made up of **fatty acids** combined with **alcohol** (usually glycerol) and **insoluble**

> [!example] Overview Functions 
> 1. **Energy Supply**: can be oxidized to provide energy for cells
> 2. **Structural Components**: used in cell membranes (see [[Cell Structures]]) 
> 3. **Waterproofing**: its insoluble property creates a water-resistant barrier
> 4. **Insulation**: help retain heat or act as electrical insulator
> 5. **Protection**: surrounds delicate organs
# Types of Lipids
## Triglyceride 
> [!important]- Main Point
> This is used to store energy in animals, plants, and some bacteria. It consists of **a glycerol backbone** attached to **3 fatty acids**
> ![[triglyceride-structure-diagram.png]]

> [!example]- Function
> 1. **Long hydrocarbon tails** - Their many carbon-hydrogen bonds can be broken to release energy.
> 2. **Low mass to energy ratio** - Lots of energy can be stored in a small volume.
> 3. **Insoluble** - They do not affect the water potential of cells as they are large and non-polar.
> 4. **High ratio of hydrogen to oxygen atoms** - Triglycerides will release water when oxidised.
### Formation & Breakdown

> [!info]- Condensation
> Hydroxyl groups (OH) on the glycerol react with the Hydrogen on the three fatty acids, forming a Triglyceride & 3 $\ce{H_{2}0}$
> ![[triglyceride-formation-breakdown.png]]

> [!info]- Hydrolysis
>  3 $\ce{H_{2}0}$ molecules are added to break the ester bonds, resulting in a glycerol and 3 fatty acids
> ![[triglyceride-formation-breakdown-diagram.png]]
## Phospholipids 

> [!important]- Main Point
> Structurally similar to [[#Triglyceride]] but only has **2 fatty acids** and a **phosphate group**. This is used as a structural component to [[Cell Structures#^Cell-membrane]]. 
![[phospholipid-structure-diagram 1.png]]

When placed in water, they arrange themselves into a **bilayer** so that the hydrophobic head points toward the water and the hydrophobic tail face away from the water.
## Cholesterol

> [!important]- Main Point
> A type of lipid (sterol) that is composed of 4 rings shape attached to a hydrocarbon chain (**hydrophobic**) and a Hydroxyl group (**hydrophilic**), making it polar overall like a [[#Phospholipids]]
![[cholesterol-structure 1.png]]

> [!example]- Functions
> 
> 1. Used to make: Vitamin D, Steroid Hormones, Bile
> 2. Helps to control stability & fluidity of animal cell membranes
> 	- High temp: causes phospholipids to pack closely and reduce fluidity
> 	- Low temp: prevents phospholipids from packing too closely and maintain more fluidity
> ![[cholesterol-inbetween-phospholipids 1.png]]

# Types of Fatty Acids
Most lipids are made up of fatty acids combined with alcohol. Fatty acids consists or a carboxyl-group (-COOH) attached to hydrocarbon chain (R group).
![[fatty-acid-structure-diagram.png]]

> [!info]- SATURATED
>- Fully saturated with Hydrogen (Carbons bonded to **maximum** number of Hydrogens)
>	- Which implies no Carbon-Carbon double bonds
>- Chains are **straight** (packed close together)
>	- Results in **high melting points** (become **solid** at room temp)
>![[saturated-fatty-acid-structure.png]]

> [!INFO]- UNSATURATED
> - Not fully saturated with Hydrogen
> - Has Carbon-Carbon Double Bonds, causing it to kink
> 	- 1-bond: Monounsaturated fatty acid
> 	- More than 1: Polyunsaturated fatty avid
> - Because the chains kink, they have **lower melting points** (become liquid at room temp)
> ![[unsaturated-fatty-acid-structure.png]]
